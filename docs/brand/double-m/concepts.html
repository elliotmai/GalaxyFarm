Brand concepts · issue #26

Double M Brand Sheet

Ten ways to burn two M’s, drawn in the same livestock-brand grammar as the approved Rocking Double Star and coloured from the locked palette in packages/config/tailwind.preset.ts. Nothing here replaces the star mark — these are candidates for the day the farm and business names land.

Every mark is shown on both real grounds, because theme is fixed per surface: Midnight Nebula for /admin and /kiosk, Bluebonnet Linen for /account, /book, /sitter and public pages. The verdict chip answers the one question the brand README already put on the record — does it still hold together at 24 px?

  • Rocking — set on a curve
  • Bar — over or under a line
  • Connected — sharing a stroke
  • Lazy — lying on its side
  • Crazy — turned upside down
  • Circle / Box / Diamond — enclosed

The ten

Read each name the way a stockman would say it aloud — the modifier first, the letter last.

Rocking Double M

Tight at 24px
Says
Straight translation of the mark already approved: same rocker, same shared ground, letters where the stars were. The least disruptive thing on this sheet.
Watch
Three elements to resolve instead of two. Below about 32 px the gap between the M’s closes and the pair reads as one long zigzag sitting on a smile.

Connected Double M

Holds at 24px
Says
The stockman’s default. One unbroken zigzag — three peaks, two valleys — two M’s sharing a middle apex. This is what a brand inspector would draw if you told them “Double M” and nothing else.
Watch
Roughly 2:1. Fine in a nav bar, awkward in the square slots this repo already ships: icon-512-maskable.png, apple-icon.png, the favicon.

Dam & Calf Double M

Tight at 24px
Says
The current mark’s whole argument, kept intact: a big M and a small one at side, on shared ground. “Double M” and “cow and calf” turn out to be the same picture.
Watch
The calf has to stay near 52% — the ratio the stars already settled — or it stops being a calf and becomes a smudge. Same design question, second time asked.

Bar Double M

Holds at 24px
Says
Connected zigzag over a straight bar. The bar does the rocker’s job — holding the letters down as one mark — with one less curve to resolve, so it survives further down the size scale than anything else here.
Watch
It trades the rocker’s warmth for precision. Reads more brand registry than homestead, which may be exactly right on an invoice and slightly cold on the kiosk.
64 40 24

Stacked Double M

Tight at 24px
Says
One M over another. The only variant that genuinely fills a square, and the only one where two tones say something true: the farm name over the business name, which issue #26 may well decide are different names.
Watch
Two-tone breaks the mono-tone rule the brand README sets. That rule was an argument about cow and calf being the same kind of thing — two businesses are not, so the exception is arguable rather than sloppy.
Also
Halving the height costs it the small end: at 24 px the two M’s merge into one blob. That is survivable, because the slots this variant exists for are 180 px and up.

Circle Double M

Tight at 24px
Says
Enclosed, so it has an edge of its own. This is the seal — the mark for the signed liability PDF and the invoice, where a logo needs to look like it was stamped rather than placed.
Watch
The ring eats the letters below about 40 px. Treat it as a document mark that lives alongside the primary, not as a candidate for the nav bar.
64 40 24

Constellation Double M

Fails at 24px
Says
The zigzag drawn as a star chart — seven stars, the three peaks brighter than the rest. It makes the logo and the pedigree screens the same visual idea, which is the strongest continuity argument on this sheet.
Watch
Genuinely beautiful at 96 px on midnight and genuinely gone at 24. It needs a solid companion below about 48 px, so picking it means shipping two drawings, not one.
64 40 24

Longhorn Double M

Holds at 24px
Says
The connected zigzag with its outer legs swept out into horn tips. Says cattle before it says letters, which is the right order for a show-calf operation that has to look like it knows the business.
Watch
The sweep is the entire idea, and it is the first thing to disappear when the stroke has to thicken for small sizes. Check it at 24 before falling for it at 96.
64 40 24

Lazy Double M

Tight at 24px
Says
Two M’s lying down, back to back — “lazy” in brand grammar. Perfectly symmetric, and it reads as a working chute, which is a nice accident for a boarding business.
Watch
The honest risk: it may not read as M at all. Ornament first, letters second. Include it so the shortlist has one mark that isn’t a zigzag, then be willing to cut it.

Double M Star

Tight at 24px
Says
The letters under a single star. The bridge mark: it concedes that the star has already earned its place and the name simply needs saying out loud next to it.
Watch
Two ideas competing for the same 24 px. Works from 40 px up; below that, one of them has to go — and the star is the one with equity.

All ten at 24 px

The nav-bar test, on the admin ground where small sizes actually live. Squint: whatever still has a shape at this size is a real candidate, and whatever turns to lint is a hero mark that needs a small companion.

If you want a recommendation

Not one mark — a family of three. This app has a 24 px nav, a 512 px maskable icon and a PDF that gets signed, and no single drawing is good at all three.

Primary

Bar Double M

Survives the nav bar, stays one glyph, and keeps a horizontal ground element as an echo of the rocker. The safest thing that is still interesting.

Square lockup

Stacked Double M

For the PWA and Apple touch icons, where the primary’s 2:1 proportion would have to be letterboxed into a square and lose half its presence. Not the favicon — at 16 px the primary still wins.

Hero & documents

Constellation Double M

For the kiosk splash, the login screen and the top of a PDF — anywhere it renders at 64 px or more on midnight, where it ties the logo to the pedigree screens.

One thing to settle first, whichever mark wins

The two shipped files disagree about colour roles, so any new mark inherits an unresolved question. rocking-double-star-customer.svg draws the stars in identity purple with the rocker in bluebonnet blue, while logomark.tsx draws the stars in --gf-text with the rocker in --gf-identity — which is what the admin file does. The component and the customer SVG therefore render different marks on a light ground.

These concepts follow the component: the letters take the text colour on midnight and identity purple on linen, and the supporting element — rocker, bar, ring, star — takes the second colour. Worth deciding deliberately rather than inheriting, since the README’s own description matches neither file exactly.
